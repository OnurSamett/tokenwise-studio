How it works

One orchestrator. A whole team of models.

Grepping a repo doesn't need your strongest model. Debugging a race condition does. tokenwise makes that call for every piece of the job.

01

Describe the task

Type what you want in plain words. The orchestrator (Claude) reads the project and splits the work.

02

Right model, right effort

Each piece goes to the cheapest worker that can do it well, in its own git worktree.

search · summariesHaiku · low
routine codeCodex Sol · medium
hard bugs · designOpus · high
03

Review, then apply

Nothing touches your project until you approve it. Per-file diffs, one click to apply, one to commit.

Features

Built to stretch every plan.

⇄

Automatic fallback

A worker near its limit or a rate-limit error moves the job to the next one: Codex, Gemini, Claude, then free models. The orchestrator has a fallback too.

↪

Orchestrator never stops

When Claude is at 90% of its 5-hour or weekly limit, or hits it mid-turn, Codex takes over the same message, read-only, with the same studio tools and the conversation so far.

◎

Model and effort, separately

Opus at low for a quick judgment call, Sonnet at high for a big routine refactor. Effort is chosen per piece, not per session.

$0

Free models as real workers

Free NVIDIA models (GLM, DeepSeek, Mistral…) read and edit files in a sandboxed worktree, ranked by Terminal-Bench 4.0 using the score of the version actually served. Opt-in.

✓

You stay in control

The orchestrator can't edit your files. Every change comes from a worker you review before it lands.

▤

Live plan limits

See your Claude and ChatGPT 5-hour and weekly usage in the sidebar, plus who will handle your next message. Stale readings past their reset time are ignored.

⚙

Pick your team

Automatic mode, or choose who writes code, who reviews and who draws images, with Best quality, Balanced and Cheapest presets.

🔔

Desktop notifications

A job is ready for review, finished or failed; an orchestrator turn is done; Codex took over. Click one to open that project's studio. A bell in the sidebar turns them off.

⌘

Mac app

A native app with a Dock icon. Every project opens in its own window, and ⌘O opens another. Notifications open the right window.

FAQ

Questions

Is it free?

Yes. tokenwise and tokenwise studio are open source (MIT). They run on your machine and use the plans and CLIs you already have; there is no tokenwise account or server.

Does it save usage, really?

On small tasks the overhead is about even; the savings come from long sessions with lots of reading, where mechanical work goes to cheaper models and other plans. We publish our benchmark harness so you can measure it on your own work.

Will my code be sent to free providers?

Only if you opt in. Free models are off until you add a key and set TOKENWISE_ALLOW_FREE=1, because free tiers may use prompts for training. They also never see secrets like .env files or keys.

Can a worker break my project?

Workers that edit files work in their own git worktree. Nothing reaches your project until you review the diff and press Apply, and applied changes are staged, not committed.

What happens when Claude runs out?

When Claude is at 90% or more of its 5-hour or weekly limit, or hits it mid-turn, Codex takes over the same message. It is read-only, has the same studio tools and sees the conversation so far. The chat shows "↪ Codex took over · Claude hit its usage limit", and the sidebar card shows who handles the next message. Turn this off with TOKENWISE_ORCH_FALLBACK=0. Gemini can't take over as orchestrator, because Antigravity has no per-session MCP; it still works as a worker.

Do notifications need anything?

macOS only. The first notification asks for permission, and tokenwise-studio setup triggers that prompt. After that, the tokenwise studio app notifies you when a job is ready for review, finished or failed, once per orchestrator turn in chats, and when Codex takes over. Clicking one opens that project's studio. The bell in the sidebar turns them off.

What do I need?

Node 20+, git, and at least one of Claude Code, the Codex CLI or Google Antigravity, signed in. macOS is the most tested platform. On macOS, setup builds the app with the Xcode command line tools if they are installed; otherwise the studio opens in the browser. Other platforms run it in the browser.
